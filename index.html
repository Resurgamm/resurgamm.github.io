Research Interests

My research interest, Cognition-Inspired Multimodal Learning, stems from a simple question: how do humans acquire knowledge from limited observations, form abstractions, and progressively learn about the world? My answer is cognition. Starting from vague blurs, infants learn to parse geometry, materials, and dynamics simply by interacting with the physical world. In my opinion, cognition is an intelligent solution to large-scale pattern recognition, integrating action and perception, and adaptive decision-making in complex environments. I wonder and would like to explore how human-like cognition mechanisms can make models implicitly simulate physical dynamics and future states and enhance models’ abilities in visual & physical reasoning.

My research explores this phenomenon through three interconnected themes.

  • Visual & Physical Cognition (MLLMs, World Models, etc.):
    Investigating human-like visual cognitive mechanisms (e.g., mind's eye) to enable multimodal models to reason over physical dynamics and abstract concepts beyond superficial pattern matching.

  • Social & Communicative Cognition (Multi-Agent Frameworks):
    Investigating socio-cognitive mechanisms, including Theory of Mind (ToM), perspective-taking, and joint attention. Exploring how multiple embodied agents, each with limited observations, can interact and communicate effectively to achieve collective reasoning and adaptive decision-making grounded in shared complex physical contexts.

  • Developmental Cognition (Development Modeling & AI for Pediatrics):
    Bridging computational models with real-world developmental data (e.g., pediatric behavior), using insights from child development to inspire AI while developing clinical tools to assess and support early human growth.
